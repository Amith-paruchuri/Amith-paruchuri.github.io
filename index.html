My relationship with bioinformatics began with resentment, not fascination. Genes felt like a kind of quiet tyranny, predetermining everything from skin color to height to disease risk, long before a person had any say in the matter. That determinism unsettled me.

Slowly, that aversion turned into fascination once I began actually working with genetic data and realized genes were never meant to be read in isolation to begin with. Disease reveals itself differently across modalities — transcriptomic activity, tissue architecture, imaging — and it was this realization that pulled me toward multimodal data fusion as the problem I actually wanted to spend my career on.

I am a final year medical student at AIIMS New Delhi, India's top medical college, which I joined after securing Rank 43 among over 20 lakh candidates in NEET 2021 on a full scholarship. I am a Khorana Scholar, a distinction awarded to ten medical students across India each year, and spent a summer at the MIND Data Science Lab at Harvard Medical School studying astrocyte transcriptomic signatures in Alzheimer's disease and tauopathies using single-cell RNA sequencing.

Since then, my work has spanned computational pathology, genomics, proteomics, and imaging — all oriented around the same question: how do we combine these fragmented biological signals into models that actually reflect how disease behaves in a real patient. I co-founded Aeternix alongside this research, working to solve the same fragmentation problem at the level of hospital data infrastructure in India.

Research Projects

ECG-derived cardiac biological age gap AHA 2026

A 1D residual neural network trained on normal ECGs from PTB-XL (n=21,837) predicts chronological age from raw 12-lead waveforms. Age-adjusted gap analysis showed significantly elevated gaps in conduction disorders, ST/T-wave changes, and myocardial infarction. Females showed consistently larger gaps than males. V2 and aVL lead skewness emerged as the dominant waveform correlates, outperforming amplitude-based features.

Python PyTorch 1D ResNet SciPy Pandas
Transcriptomic drivers of MASLD progression — SPP1 as a central network hub ACG 2026

RNA-seq from 216 liver biopsies (control, NAFL, NASH stratified F0–F4) identified a 563-gene fibroinflammatory progression signature via voom-limma. GSEA showed enrichment of EMT, TNF/NF-κB, and IL-6/STAT3 pathways with suppression of oxidative phosphorylation. STRING network analysis identified SPP1 (osteopontin) as the dominant hub gene — betweenness 476, over double the next node — rising progressively with fibrosis stage (FDR 2.9e-9).

R voom-limma GSEA clusterProfiler STRING
Brain–retina biological aging coupling AAIC 2026

Independent aging clocks were derived from retinal fundus imaging using pretrained foundation models and from structural brain imaging features. Residualized modeling, interaction analysis, and spline-based nonlinear regression tested whether age-gap deviations converge or diverge across the lifespan, demonstrating how multimodal imaging can capture shared systemic aging biology.

Python PyTorch NumPy SciPy Matplotlib
Astrocyte transcriptomic signatures in Alzheimer's disease and tauopathies AAIC 2026

Single-nucleus RNA sequencing was used to identify shared and disease-specific astrocyte states across Alzheimer's disease and primary tauopathies. Clustering and differential proportion analysis revealed distinct astrocyte phenotypes enriched in inflammatory, stress signaling, and homeostatic pathways. Spatial transcriptomic mapping localized reactive phenotypes to disease-relevant brain regions.

R Seurat SCENIC clusterProfiler
Lymphocyte infiltration at tumor border in DLBCL USCAP 2026

Tumor and cellular compartments were segmented in DLBCL whole-slide images using deep learning, and a transition zone band was created around tumor margins. Spatial infiltration patterns were modeled as continuous curves, with slope-based features extracted to capture tumor–immune topology not visible through bulk immune density measures.

Python TIAToolbox CellViT OpenCV

VUS landscape in Indian metastatic renal cell carcinoma

Standard targeted genomic panels leave a large fraction of variants unclassified in renal cell carcinoma — a critical gap in populations underrepresented in global databases. This cross-sectional study uses whole-exome sequencing in histologically confirmed metastatic RCC patients enrolled at AIIMS New Delhi. Variants in RCC susceptibility and RCC-related genes are classified per ACMG guidelines, with VUS subsequently re-evaluated against IndiGenomes, an indigenous Indian population frequency database, to assess reclassification potential. The study aims to establish a VUS-specific genomic reference for Indian RCC patients and explore the clinical significance of previously uncharacterized variants.

Whole-exome sequencing ACMG classification IndiGenomes R
